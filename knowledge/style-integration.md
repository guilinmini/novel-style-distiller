# Distilled style integration

Use this module when mapping the locked style contract onto a chapter, diagnosing drift, or avoiding mechanical repetition.

## Three layers

Apply style through three distinct layers:

1. **Invariants**: POV access, tense, narrative stance, distance range, register, and hard prohibitions.
2. **Scene modes**: action, dialogue, introspection, transition, climax, or project-defined modes that change local intensity.
3. **Accents**: occasional imagery, fragments, repetitions, or paragraph effects used only under their recorded conditions.

Invariants remain stable. Modes follow scene function. Accents must not become compulsory decoration.

## Style selection per scene

Before drafting, record:

- scene function and emotional movement;
- active style mode;
- two or three controls to emphasize;
- one likely overuse symptom;
- any deliberate deviation and its reason.

Do not attempt to maximize every feature in the contract. The result should remain recognizable across different scene types without having identical cadence.

## Character voice versus narrative voice

The contract governs the work's narrative system. Dialogue still follows each character's vocabulary, tactics, knowledge, and pressure response. A stable narrator must not flatten all speakers into one voice.

## Drift detection

Compare the chapter to contract checks, not to source prose. Common drift patterns:

- one easy surface marker replaces deeper information or emotion mechanisms;
- paragraph and sentence rhythm becomes uniform;
- the narrator explains more than the POV contract permits;
- imagery domains repeat without scene function;
- dialogue voices converge;
- intense modes persist through recovery and transition scenes;
- generic craft advice overrides the distilled contract.

Repair by returning to the exact observable control and scene condition. Never reopen the source novel during runtime writing.

## Long-horizon calibration

At chapter, arc, and volume boundaries:

1. sample chapters with different functions;
2. check invariants across all samples;
3. verify mode variation rather than surface sameness;
4. identify recurring openings, endings, metaphors, gestures, and sentence patterns;
5. record corrections without silently changing the locked contract version.

If the contract itself needs revision, create a new pack version and run regression checks before upgrading the project.
