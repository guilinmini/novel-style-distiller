# Story architecture for long-form fiction

Use this module when establishing or changing the premise, ending, act/volume structure, central conflict, or macro escalation.

## Story engine

A durable long-form engine needs all five elements:

```text
desire + opposition + recurring decision pressure + accumulating cost + irreversible change
```

If chapters can be reordered without changing consequences, the engine is producing episodes rather than a causal novel. Repair by making the result of one major choice constrain the next.

## Promise, question, and ending

Define before expanding the outline:

- **Reader promise**: the kind of experience and reward the genre/setup commits to deliver.
- **Central dramatic question**: a question answerable through character action, not theme exposition.
- **Ending state**: who or what has irreversibly changed.
- **Climactic choice**: the decision that causes the ending rather than merely witnessing it.
- **Thematic consequence**: what the outcome demonstrates, including its cost.

An ending direction may remain flexible, but the outline needs a pressure-bearing destination. “The protagonist grows” is not an ending state.

## Causal spine

For each macro turn, record:

| Field | Test |
|---|---|
| Cause | Which prior action, failure, or revelation made this likely? |
| Choice | Who chooses, and what viable alternative was rejected? |
| Cost | What resource, relationship, belief, safety, or opportunity is lost? |
| Constraint | How does the result narrow later options? |
| New pressure | Why can the story not return to its prior equilibrium? |

Coincidence can create trouble; it should not repeatedly solve trouble.

## Act and volume units

Each act or volume should have its own temporary engine:

```text
entry imbalance → local pursuit → complication ladder → irreversible turn → changed exit state
```

The unit must advance the central arc while delivering a partial reward. A volume that only postpones the main question is filler, even if individual scenes are exciting.

For very long projects, use nested planning horizons:

- lock the ending direction and current volume;
- detail the next 3–8 chapters;
- keep later chapters at functional-summary level;
- revise distant plans only after accepted events change their assumptions.

## Escalation dimensions

Do not escalate only by making enemies stronger. Rotate among:

- narrower time or spatial options;
- higher personal or moral cost;
- damaged alliances and conflicting obligations;
- new information that reframes earlier choices;
- resource depletion or increased visibility;
- success that creates a harder responsibility.

Escalation is valid when it changes what the protagonist must risk or decide.

## Structural diagnostics

| Symptom | Likely cause | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Strong opening, inert middle | Central goal has no changing constraints | Add decision-caused consequences and intermediate irreversible turns |
| Endless new villains/problems | Pressure is added rather than compounded | Reuse consequences, relationships, and unpaid promises |
| Twist feels arbitrary | Reveal has no prior causal or informational footprint | Plant fair evidence and connect the twist to a character choice |
| Climax feels external | Protagonist does not cause the decisive outcome | Rebuild around the arc's hardest choice and cost |
| Ending explains the theme | Theme was not embodied in consequences | Make the final state and sacrifice carry the meaning |
