# Gratification, progression, and escalation

Use this module when designing a “爽点” engine, balancing suppression and payoff, planning visible progression, or repairing chapters that contain events but no reader reward.

## The earned reward loop

A durable gratification beat usually contains:

```text
legible desire or pressure
→ anticipation and a credible obstacle
→ protagonist choice, preparation, or competence
→ resistance or price
→ decisive turn
→ concrete result
→ recognition / emotional aftershock
→ new option, responsibility, enemy, or ceiling
```

Not every beat needs public recognition, but the result must change something observable. Internal relief without changed action or relationship is rarely enough for a promised major payoff.

## Reward families

Vary the operative pleasure:

| Family | Concrete result | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| competence | a difficult task is solved through demonstrated ability | narration declares genius without a hard problem |
| status | rank, reputation, access, or authority changes | disposable spectators repeat praise |
| resource | money, equipment, territory, time, or information is secured | gain has no later use or cost |
| discovery | a useful truth or rule changes strategy | trivia does not alter options |
| justice/reversal | prior pressure is answered through causally earned action | humiliation is prolonged only to inflate retaliation |
| relationship | trust, loyalty, intimacy, or allegiance changes | affection arrives without choice or risk |
| survival | escape or endurance preserves something at real cost | danger resets with no consequence |
| progression | a capability or threshold visibly improves | numbers rise but available decisions do not change |
| rule exploitation | the protagonist uses a system consequence creatively | loophole makes all future resistance irrelevant |

## Intensity ladder

- **micro**: useful information, a small edge, a witty reversal, one person's changed response;
- **small**: a scene objective won, a tool acquired, a local opponent answered;
- **medium**: a multi-chapter promise paid, threshold crossed, relationship/status materially changed;
- **major**: an arc engine turns, a major rival falls, a scarce capability or position is earned;
- **arc**: the local genre promise resolves and creates the next scale of story.

Do not increase only spectacle. Escalate what the protagonist must risk, decide, protect, understand, or become responsible for.

## Suppression budget

Pressure creates appetite only when readers can see a plausible path to agency. Track the debt:

- What was denied or threatened?
- What action can eventually answer it?
- What evidence says the story remembers the promise?
- What is the latest acceptable payoff window?
- What smaller reward keeps the interval alive?

Repeated humiliation without agency is not automatically stronger setup. If suppression debt grows while payoff windows move, either pay, reframe with genuine value, or cut the cycle.

## Recognition without cardboard spectators

Choose observers whose changed behavior matters: a rival alters tactics, an institution grants access, an ally revises trust, a dependent gains hope, or the protagonist privately revises self-belief. Avoid crowds that exist only to announce the intended emotion.

## Escalation checks

- The reward changes later options rather than decorating the current chapter.
- The protagonist contributes a choice, cost, or competence.
- A new tier preserves old consequences instead of resetting them.
- Counters attack the engine's limits, not arbitrary immunity.
- Recent chapters vary reward family, visibility, and intensity.
- The next pressure follows from the payoff rather than canceling it.

Record planned and delivered cycles in `state/REWARD_LEDGER.md`, and mirror only active near-term pressure in `state/CURRENT_STATE.md`.
