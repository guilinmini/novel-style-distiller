# Web-fiction positioning and the opening three chapters

Use this module when turning a theme into a Chinese serialized web-fiction project, planning chapters 1–3, or repairing an opening that explains much but gives readers little reason to continue.

## Position before plotting

Write one concrete sentence for each item:

- **reader fantasy**: what recurring experience the reader is buying;
- **protagonist engine**: what lets the protagonist act differently from ordinary people;
- **resistance engine**: what prevents that advantage from ending the story immediately;
- **progression proof**: what observable state can improve over time;
- **long promise**: what larger identity, status, relationship, truth, or world position can eventually change;
- **first-cycle payoff**: the first small result that proves the story can deliver its promise.

Do not confuse a setting label with a sell. “Cultivation world” names a setting; “an exiled physician turns forbidden diagnoses into cultivation breakthroughs while every cure exposes him to the tribunal” identifies action, advantage, resistance, and cost.

## The opening-three-chapter contract

“Golden three chapters” is a retention contract, not a mandatory beat count. Each chapter must do distinct work.

### Chapter 1: establish pressure and a credible promise

- Put the protagonist in a specific imbalance, threat, humiliation, need, or opportunity.
- Show an active choice before extended explanation.
- Reveal or strongly imply the protagonist engine.
- Deliver one micro-result or sharp reversal; do not postpone all satisfaction.
- End with a changed option, obligation, target, or danger.

### Chapter 2: prove the engine works under resistance

- Let the protagonist pursue a concrete objective using choice or competence.
- Add a rule, cost, rival response, or knowledge limit that prevents effortless repetition.
- Make the result visible to at least one meaningful observer when status is part of the fantasy.
- Pay or materially advance the strongest promise from chapter 1.
- Open a larger but causally connected opportunity or threat.

### Chapter 3: complete the first reward cycle

- Force a harder use of the protagonist engine.
- Deliver the first small but complete payoff: gain, reversal, recognition, escape, discovery, justice, relationship movement, or threshold crossing.
- Preserve a consequence or new ceiling so the payoff does not reset the story.
- Clarify the next short arc and why the reader should expect a larger version of the promised experience.

The three chapters may cover a different number of scenes, and slow-burn genres may use quieter rewards. The test is whether pressure, agency, proof, and forward promise are all observable.

## Opening information budget

Introduce a fact only when it changes the current goal, risk, tactic, interpretation, or reward. Prefer:

- a rule discovered through attempted action;
- status shown through treatment and access;
- backstory exposed by a present cost;
- terminology anchored to a visible consequence.

Delay genealogies, system catalogs, faction histories, and decorative geography until a decision needs them.

## Opening failure checks

| Failure | Diagnostic | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Premise without protagonist action | Things happen around the lead | Give the lead a consequential choice in chapter 1 |
| Advantage without resistance | Any future problem looks trivial | Establish cost, counterforce, limit, or exposure |
| Three chapters of suppression | The promised pleasure never arrives | Deliver a complete small payoff by chapter 3 |
| Random cliffhangers | New danger is unrelated to prior action | Make the exit pressure a consequence of the chapter |
| Setting lecture | Removing paragraphs changes no decision | Move facts to the scene where they alter a tactic |
| False scale | Chapter 1 begins at maximum spectacle | Start with a legible personal stake and leave escalation room |

## Project writeback

Record the positioning contract in `NOVEL_PROJECT.md`, the progression rules in the Story Bible, the first reward cycle in `state/REWARD_LEDGER.md`, and the chapter-level hook/payoff obligations in `state/SERIAL_RHYTHM.md` and `state/PLOT_THREADS.md`.
