# Scene and chapter design

Use this module when creating a chapter blueprint, repairing a flat scene, choosing entry/exit points, or balancing action with aftermath.

## Scene contract

Before drafting, define:

- POV owner and their immediate goal;
- concrete opposition or friction;
- what can be lost, exposed, delayed, or changed;
- the information permissions at entry;
- the turn that changes the scene's direction;
- the exit state that differs from the entry state.

A scene may be quiet, but it cannot be inert. Its change can be external, relational, informational, emotional, or strategic.

## Functional beat sequence

Use as a diagnostic, not a formula:

```text
orientation → pursuit/tactic → resistance → adjustment
→ turn/reveal/choice → consequence → exit pressure
```

Skip orientation already supplied by the previous chapter. Compress repeated tactics. Give the turn enough consequence that the next scene inherits a changed condition.

## Scene and sequel

After high-impact action, readers often need a processing unit:

```text
reaction → interpretation → options → decision
```

This can occupy a full scene, a paragraph, or be delayed. Remove it only when confusion or emotional suppression is intentional. Too many action units cause weightlessness; too many processing units cause drift.

## Chapter architecture

A chapter should have a local question or pressure, a sequence of scene changes, and an exit that changes reading momentum. Common exit functions include:

- a decision that commits the next action;
- a revelation that reframes the goal;
- a cost that removes an easy option;
- a relationship shift;
- a partial answer that opens a sharper question;
- deliberate closure after a promised emotional payoff.

Not every chapter needs a cliffhanger. Every chapter needs a reason its boundary belongs where it is.

## Entry and exit

Enter after routine setup when the meaningful pressure begins. Exit after the consequence becomes clear enough to alter expectation. Avoid habitually opening with waking/travel and closing with surprise unless those actions carry specific story change.

## Scene audit

| Question | Failure signal |
|---|---|
| What changes? | The summary before and after is identical |
| Who drives it? | The POV character only receives exposition |
| Why now? | The scene could occur anywhere in the novel |
| What is withheld? | Everyone states all relevant information immediately |
| What carries forward? | No later scene depends on this result |
| Why this length? | Word count follows habit rather than dramatic function |
