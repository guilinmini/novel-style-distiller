# Worldbuilding and exposition

Use this module when creating or changing world rules, magic/technology systems, institutions, factions, terminology, or the delivery of setting information.

## Rule design

For every story-relevant rule define:

- capability: what it makes possible;
- limit: where it stops working;
- cost: what use consumes or risks;
- access: who can use, know, regulate, or exploit it;
- consequence: how it changes daily behavior and power;
- exception policy: whether exceptions exist and how readers can recognize them.

A rule matters when characters can make decisions with it and suffer consequences from it. Decorative lore belongs in background notes, not the active Bible.

## Systemic consequences

If a major capability exists, test its effects on:

- work and material life;
- law, authority, status, and inequality;
- travel, communication, medicine, conflict, and trade;
- belief, ritual, taboo, education, and language;
- who benefits from keeping the system misunderstood.

Do not invent every consequence. Record the few that affect the central story and keep them consistent.

## Institutions and factions

A functioning institution needs:

- public purpose;
- actual incentives;
- resources and enforcement;
- internal disagreement;
- rules for membership and exclusion;
- dependency on other groups;
- a way its structure pressures characters.

Avoid factions whose members all share one opinion or whose resources appear only when the plot needs them.

## Exposition through use

Reveal information when it changes a current goal, tactic, risk, interpretation, or relationship. Useful delivery forms include:

- a rule enforced with consequence;
- a task whose steps expose constraints;
- disagreement between people with different access;
- a failed assumption;
- an object or location in changed condition;
- a decision that requires comparing costs.

Orientation should arrive before the reader needs it, but explanation can wait until it becomes actionable.

## Terminology discipline

Give new terms one stable meaning, a limited set of variants, and enough local context for inference. Do not introduce clusters of names before the reader has a reason to distinguish them. Track renames and forbidden variants in the Bible and continuity ledger.

## Diagnostic checks

| Symptom | Repair |
|---|---|
| Rule solves new problems without prior limit | Add cost/access/precedent and revise dependent scenes |
| Long lore passage stops the chapter | Move only actionable information into conflict or task |
| World feels like a painted backdrop | Show one rule changing work, power, relationship, or choice |
| Exception feels convenient | Establish recognition conditions and pay an appropriate cost |
| Readers cannot retain terms | Reduce simultaneous terms and reinforce through function |
