# Genre promises and serialization

Use this module when defining reader expectations, planning a serialized or very long novel, renewing an arc, or diagnosing chapters that advance plot without delivering satisfaction.

## Promise stack

Record three levels:

- **primary genre promise**: the central kind of experience and final reward;
- **secondary promise**: a compatible source of texture or tension;
- **project-specific promise**: the unique question, relationship, system, or transformation established by the premise.

When promises conflict, decide which one governs the climax. Do not market one experience and consistently deliver another.

## Common promise questions

Use only the relevant row:

| Mode | Ongoing promise | Typical required payoff |
|---|---|---|
| mystery | clues can support a discoverable truth | solution follows fair evidence and changes consequences |
| romance | relationship choices can transform intimacy/trust | decisive relational choice and earned new state |
| thriller | danger and narrowing options force action | confrontation resolves the central threat at real cost |
| fantasy/science fiction | rules and altered conditions shape choices | system/world consequence affects the climax, not just décor |
| horror | exposure to threat changes safety, identity, or belief | confrontation/revelation preserves consequence, not easy reset |
| progression/adventure | effort, competence, access, and cost accumulate | visible advancement plus a larger constraint or responsibility |
| literary/character-centered | choices expose contradiction and meaning | changed self/relationship/world state embodies the thematic tension |

These are diagnostic expectations, not mandatory formulas.

## Serial chapter value

A serialized chapter should usually deliver at least one present reward while creating future pressure:

- a partial answer;
- a meaningful choice;
- competence or failure with consequence;
- relationship movement;
- access to a new place, person, tool, or truth;
- a promised confrontation or emotional beat;
- a reframe that makes earlier material more valuable.

Ending on a question does not compensate for a chapter that delivered nothing.

## Arc renewal

At the end of an arc or volume:

1. pay the local promise;
2. show aftermath and changed status;
3. preserve consequences rather than reset convenience;
4. reveal how the success/failure creates the next constraint;
5. refresh goals, relationships, and reader questions;
6. build an arc summary before expanding the next detailed horizon.

## Anti-bloat checks

- New cast, setting, faction, or system must change the active engine.
- Repeated obstacle types should produce different decisions or costs.
- Side arcs need their own payoff window and effect on the central story.
- Recap should be embedded in changed interpretation or current need, not repeated summary.
- If several chapters can be removed without changing state, merge or redesign them.
