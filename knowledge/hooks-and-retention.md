# Hook system and retention

Use this module when planning chapter entries/exits, diagnosing drop-off, varying serial momentum, or checking whether an old hook has been paid.

## A hook is an obligation

A hook changes what the reader expects to receive. It must arise from the chapter, point to a legible future event or answer, and carry a payoff window. Surprise alone is not a hook; withholding everything is not mystery.

## Thirteen functional hook families

Use the function, not a fixed sentence pattern:

1. **Immediate threat** — safety, freedom, identity, or possession is about to be lost.
2. **Unfinished action** — an initiated attempt reaches a meaningful interruption point.
3. **Irreversible decision** — the protagonist chooses a path whose consequences begin next.
4. **Revelation or reframe** — a fact changes the meaning of what the reader already saw.
5. **Exposure risk** — a secret identity, lie, capability, or relationship approaches discovery.
6. **Countdown** — a deadline gives delay a concrete cost.
7. **Reward arrival** — a promised gain, upgrade, answer, or meeting becomes immediately accessible.
8. **New price or constraint** — success reveals a cost, rule, debt, or countermeasure.
9. **Confrontation arrival** — two forces with incompatible objectives finally share the scene horizon.
10. **Question or clue** — bounded evidence creates an answerable uncertainty.
11. **Status reversal** — power, reputation, access, or social position visibly flips.
12. **Emotional rupture or commitment** — a relationship cannot return unchanged.
13. **Horizon expansion** — the local result exposes a larger arena, tier, faction, or possibility.

## Selection rules

- Match the hook to the chapter's actual change; do not append an unrelated emergency.
- Alternate families and intensity. Three consecutive surprise arrivals feel mechanical even if the visitors differ.
- A major hook needs prior investment. A micro-hook may simply clarify the next action.
- Not every chapter needs a cliffhanger. Closure plus a concrete next desire can retain readers.
- Record what is promised, not merely the hook label.

## Payoff discipline

For every meaningful hook, record:

```text
hook ID
→ created chapter and cause
→ promised answer/event/reward
→ earliest and latest credible payoff
→ current status
→ result or reason for reframe
```

The next chapter may partially pay, complicate, or credibly delay the promise. It may not silently replace it with a new hook. Reframing is valid only when the reader gains information or consequence.

## Entry hooks

A chapter opening should reconnect to live pressure quickly. Useful entries include:

- the immediate consequence of the prior exit;
- a changed routine that demonstrates the last chapter's effect;
- a goal already in motion;
- a delayed answer arriving in a new context;
- a contrast beat that lets the reader feel a recent victory or loss.

Avoid reflexively opening with waking, weather, travel recap, or unexplained danger.

## Retention audit

- Did this chapter deliver present value before asking for another click?
- Is the exit pressure caused by an action, discovery, or existing force?
- Can the reader state what they expect next?
- Is an older stronger promise overdue?
- Has the same hook family dominated the rolling window?
- Does the promised payoff remain compatible with character knowledge and canon?

Write current obligations to `state/SERIAL_RHYTHM.md`; use `state/PLOT_THREADS.md` for their longer causal dependencies.
