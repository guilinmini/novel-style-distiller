# Dialogue and subtext

Use this module for dialogue-heavy scenes, speaker differentiation, hidden objectives, interrogation, negotiation, or exposition repair.

## Dialogue as action

Before writing a conversation, give each participant:

- an immediate objective;
- what they cannot or will not say directly;
- current leverage and vulnerability;
- a preferred tactic;
- a trigger that causes the tactic to change.

A dialogue turn should attempt to alter knowledge, emotion, status, obligation, or action. Greetings and logistics may be compressed unless they carry one of those functions.

## Subtext

Subtext exists when the literal content and operative purpose differ. Build it through:

- selective answers;
- questions used as accusations, tests, offers, or delays;
- changed subject or level of specificity;
- action that contradicts speech;
- asymmetric knowledge;
- politeness under threat;
- repeated word or omission whose meaning changes.

Do not make every line cryptic. Contrast gives subtext force.

## Speaker differentiation

Separate speakers using a combination of:

- vocabulary domain and precision;
- directness and tolerance for silence;
- sentence rhythm and interruption behavior;
- preferred social tactic;
- emotional leakage pattern;
- what they notice or refuse to name.

Test by removing dialogue tags from a short exchange. If speakers become indistinguishable, revise their goals and tactics before adding verbal quirks.

## Exposition control

Characters should not explain shared knowledge for the reader. Deliver necessary information through disagreement, correction, negotiation, consequence, partial recall, or a practical task.

Ask of each explanatory line:

1. Why does this speaker say it now?
2. Why to this listener?
3. What response are they trying to produce?
4. What would they omit, distort, or emphasize?

## Dialogue scene turn

A conversation needs a changed condition: someone learns, commits, lies successfully, loses leverage, exposes a need, refuses, or forces a new tactic. End when the operative relationship or next action changes, not when all topics are exhausted.
