# Reader emotion curves and payoff

Use this module when planning a chapter group, preventing emotional monotony, timing a reward, or distinguishing protagonist emotion from reader emotion.

## Track two dimensions

- **valence**: relief/pleasure versus pain/dread;
- **activation**: calm/processing versus urgency/excitement.

High activation is not always positive, and negative emotion is not always low energy. Name the intended movement instead of writing “stronger emotion.”

## Three simultaneous curves

1. **scene curve** — moment-to-moment pressure, turn, and consequence;
2. **chapter curve** — the dominant entry state, major movement, and exit residue;
3. **arc curve** — accumulated appetite, false relief, major payoff, aftermath, and renewed desire.

A batch plan should show how chapters differ. Ten chapters of steadily rising pressure become numbness; ten chapters of equal victories become weightless.

## A practical serial wave

Use as a diagnostic, not a compulsory formula:

```text
orientation or residue
→ appetite / pressure
→ attempted agency
→ complication
→ hope, dread, anger, curiosity, or anticipation sharpens
→ turn and reward/cost
→ enough aftermath to feel the result
→ new emotional question
```

After a major payoff, preserve a processing beat. Let changed status, body, relationships, or options make the victory emotionally real before opening a larger threat.

## Emotion earns prose space through consequence

Prefer observable interpretation and altered behavior:

- a decision becomes harder or easier;
- a character avoids, confronts, bargains, confesses, or protects;
- perception narrows or notices a newly meaningful detail;
- a relationship tactic changes;
- a body or resource cost constrains the next action.

Do not rely on repeated emotion labels, universal bodily shorthand, or decorative weather.

## Batch variety check

Across the active detailed horizon, inspect:

- dominant reader emotion per chapter;
- activation pattern and recovery space;
- promised versus delivered emotional payoff;
- whether different characters generate distinct emotional pressures;
- whether the same victory/anger/shock curve repeats;
- whether an arc climax has adequate accumulated appetite.

Store the rolling chapter wave in `state/SERIAL_RHYTHM.md`. Store durable relationship or knowledge consequences in their own ledgers rather than in an emotion-only summary.
