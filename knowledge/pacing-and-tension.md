# Pacing and tension

Use this module for slow middles, rushed turns, repetitive chapters, weak suspense, or scene-length decisions.

## Separate pace from speed

- **Story speed**: how much fictional time or how many events pass.
- **Reading pace**: how quickly the prose is processed.
- **Narrative pressure**: how strongly the reader anticipates consequence.

A quiet conversation can have high pressure; a chase can feel slow if it repeats without changing options.

## Tension components

Useful tension usually combines at least two:

- a desired outcome;
- uncertainty about attaining it;
- credible consequence of failure or success;
- incomplete or asymmetric information;
- a narrowing deadline or opportunity;
- incompatible obligations;
- delayed but expected confrontation.

Do not substitute vagueness for uncertainty. Readers need enough orientation to understand what might matter.

## Compression and dilation

Compress when events are repetitive, predictable, low-choice, or primarily connective. Dilate when a decision, discovery, reversal, relationship shift, or sensory/emotional consequence needs to be experienced.

Controls include:

- scene versus summary;
- sentence and paragraph length;
- number of intermediate actions shown;
- dialogue turn count;
- interior processing duration;
- specificity of sensory focus;
- distance between setup and consequence.

Use the locked style contract to determine how these controls are expressed.

## Long-form rhythm

Track chapters by pressure function rather than a single 1–10 score:

| Function | Examples |
|---|---|
| acquire | gain information, ally, access, resource |
| spend | risk or lose a resource, trust, safety, time |
| narrow | remove options or shorten a deadline |
| reframe | change the meaning of known facts |
| confront | force incompatible goals into the same scene |
| recover | process cost and form a new decision |
| pay off | answer a promise with proportionate consequence |

Avoid long runs where every chapter performs the same function.

## Diagnostics

| Symptom | Likely issue | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Many events, little urgency | Events do not alter choices | Add consequences that narrow or conflict options |
| Constant high intensity | No contrast or processing | Add aftermath, reorientation, intimacy, or strategic quiet |
| Slow exposition | Information has no immediate use | Attach it to conflict, choice, misconception, or cost |
| Rushed emotional turn | Consequence is acknowledged but not experienced | Dilate reaction, interpretation, and changed behavior |
| Repetitive cliffhangers | Chapter exits use one mechanism | Rotate decision, cost, answer, reframe, and closure |
